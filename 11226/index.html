The Docks of New York

Quick one while I wait for this work call to be over: I skipped Quaker meeting on Sunday to catch Docks of New York at the Music Box with Loren and Sophie. It was the first time I'd missed a meeting since I started going again last April. It felt a bit strange. I always try to fit quiet, worshipful time into my week, but there really is no substitute for the work we do together on Sundays. Even still, I could not say no to the opportunity to see some delicious Sternberg light & shadow on the big screen. I was not disappointed :) what a picture..George Bancroft lugs himself to and fro across some of the most beautiful and dynamic blocking I've ever seen in a silent film, raining down slapstick wrath on anybody who's even a little bit in his way. It is so silly, and yet there is such a powerful tenderness running through the whole thing. The love is in the doing, not the saying. Bill and Mae's marriage, rough and unworkable and completely legally illegitimate, confronts the impossible in a way that melts my heart. We are married. We are doomed. I would kill a man to bring you a hot toddy. Let me mend your jacket, you can't go to sea like that.. I walked away from the whole thing with the idea that maybe there is no such thing as a hollow ritual.

Visually, well, what can you even say. Go watch the first 10 minutes right now. Actually I'm embedding the entire movie into this blog post. You have no excuse. Just click play and sit there for 10 minutes.

Those murky shadows! Nothing like it..
