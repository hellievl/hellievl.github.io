The Kenaissance

Summer ended precisely one week ago in Chicago, which means blogging is once again required to maintain residency in the Corridor. Much to discuss..but today I am mostly thinking about Ken Russell. The Devils was just rereleased as a fancy new director’s cut on a new print. Everyone is going nuts for it. Naturally, it is my pleasure and my privilege to put on my fun-hating contrarian hat and say “you are all liking this thing I like incorrectly”.

Ken’s curse is that he is a fundamentally bad filmmaker who, on more than one occasion, stumbled upon some extremely fertile ground in pursuit of stupid misogynistic bullshit. Nothing that he thinks makes his movies good is what makes them good. Ken built his mainstream success off a reputation as a “provocateur”. He is famous for filling his movies with sex, violence, psychedelic substances, blasphemy, and other generally “scandalous” behavior. Yawn! The Devils is more or less what kicked this off. Ken released 3 movies in 1971: The Music Lovers, The Boy Friend, and The Devils. Two of these represent his best work, and one of them made a lot of money. The Devils made a lot of money. It is also the most “scandalous” of the three (what if a nun JERKED OFF!!) Ken certainly noticed this, and started leaning into this angle in future films, which grew increasingly bombastic as time went on. Ken’s Late Style is as outrageous as it is pathetic. Shout out Dog Boys, his lusty made for TV dog training prison western (not his worst!)

The Devils rerelease, coupled with the ongoing Russell series at Doc Films (which is mostly his stinkers) signals to me that we’re on the verge of a Kenaissance. I worry it is happening for all the wrong reasons. The people are crying out for ridiculous films they can guffaw at and write pithy Letterboxd reviews about. They should be crying out for the SENSUAL! Though he doesn’t quite know it, Ken understands the sensual. He understands feeling a big feeling in your body and completely lacking the ability to communicate it- the desperation of inexpressibility. Ira summed it up with “he's interested in when a feeling possesses your whole body and makes you exhibit behaviors !!!!!!”. The other two films he released in ‘71 are perfect in their understanding of this. Of course, this sort of thing is present in all his films to some degree, but it’s hard to appreciate it when everything is bogged down in swastikas and mushroom trips and gulp…..sex workers.*

To be clear, I am not saying The Devils is a bad movie, I actually think it’s pretty good, but I take its enduring popularity as a sign that modern day Ken-heads are missing the point. There’s also the largely unaddressed veneer of woman-hating over every single one of his films. Pesky women are always causing problems in the Russellverse.** This is inadvertently used to spellbinding effect in The Music Lovers, where closeted Tchaikovsky is so scared of pussy that he causes his wife to suffer a psychotic break and then kills himself. But doesn’t it feel like that sometimes…! Nowhere else have I seen the histrionic desire that once hovered over my life so clearly rendered on screen. Hang on, why am I not writing an essay about that. Soon :) Anyway, my point is, more than once, he totally failed to realize he was touching on the more terrifying and beautiful parts of being alive because he was too distracted by women forcing men to commit unspeakable acts by..being women, and people seem to love this for some reason. I feel like I had a better ending for this and then I had to run to a meeting and now the day is over and I would really like to go home <3 Screed over

*He actually has two prudishly sensational sex work films, and would you believe they are both really bad. WHORE (1991) was marketed with the tagline “If you can’t say it, see it” lol

**His misogyny, always present, got particularly bad in the 80s. Gothic (1986) and Altered States (1980) are genuinely heinous in their treatment of women from start to finish. Poor Mary Shelley :(
